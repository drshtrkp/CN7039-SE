IS Foundations, Feasibility Math & Root-Cause Analysis

Page 3 of 15
CN7039 Week 1 PPTs & LabsTextbook 8e Ch. 1 & 3

A System takes input → processes it → produces output. An Information System turns raw Data (`40, London`) into meaningful Information (`Mark lives in Bristol`). Think of the Consultant Effect: a doctor who only prescribes painkillers for a headache treats symptoms; a good analyst finds the root cause (infection).

  • Stakeholders: System Owners (sponsors/pay), System Users (Internal/External, Remote, Mobile), System Designers (blueprints), System Builders (code), Analysts (Business vs System Analyst).
  • 5 Business Drivers: Globalization, E-Commerce (B2C/B2B) vs E-Business, Knowledge Asset Management, CPI & TQM, Business Process Redesign (BPR).
  • Enterprise Apps: ERP (integrated company suite), SCM (`Suppliers → Processes → Products`), CRM (inquiry to post-sale support).
  • Project vs Process Mgmt: Single project time/budget vs ongoing methodology standards across all projects.
  • [Textbook Ch. 1] 3 Feasibilities & Financial Formulas: Technical (Can we build it?), Economic (Should we?), Organizational (Will they use it?).
    • ROI = (Total Benefits - Total Costs) / Total Costs
    • PV = CashFlow / (1 + r)^n; NPV = Sum(PV Benefits) - Sum(PV Costs) (accept if NPV > 0)
    • Break-Even Point = Years negative + (NetCashFlow - CumCashFlow) / NetCashFlow
  • [Textbook Ch. 3] Functional vs Nonfunctional Requirements: Process/Information needed vs Operational, Performance, Security, Cultural/Political constraints.
  • Worked Cases: Supermarket 12% expiry loss (root cause: manual checks + no upward reporting); 18-floor lifts (root cause: naive nearest-lift stranding lifts after rush hour).

CN7039 Week 2 PPTs & Med Kits CaseTextbook 8e Ch. 2, 6, 11–13

Linear = one-way street (no going back). Waterfall = river where you can paddle back upstream if you find an error. CBD reuses pre-built software blocks instead of coding from scratch.

  • 7 Phases: Planning (CSF, Scope, Plan, Milestones) → Analysis (JAD, Requirements, Sign-off) → Design (Tech Arch, System Model) → Development → Testing (Unit, Integration, System, UAT) → Implementation (Parallel, Plunge, Pilot, Phased; Online/Workshop training) → Maintenance (Help desk).
  • Sourcing: Insourcing (sensitive/unique), Selfsourcing (reduces invisible backlog; never for ERP/infra), Outsourcing (4 choices; risk of IP exposure/dependency).
  • CBD & Prototyping: RAD, XP (tiny phases), Agile; Proof-of-concept & Selling prototypes.
  • [Textbook Ch. 2] System Prototyping vs Throwaway Prototyping: System prototype evolves into final system (fast, but risks weak analysis); Throwaway (design) prototype explores unclear tech/UI and is discarded before real build (more stable).
  • [Textbook Ch. 11–12] Testing & Conversion: Black-box (from specs) vs White-box (reads code); Stubs simulate unfinished modules; Alpha (fake data) vs Beta (real data). Conversion across 3 dimensions: Style (Direct/Parallel), Location (Pilot/Phased/Simultaneous), Modules (Whole/Modular).
  • [Textbook Ch. 13] Scrum Framework: Product Owner (backlog priority), ScrumMaster (servant leader removing blockers), Dev Team (5–9 self-organizing). Sprints (1–4 wks), User Stories (As a..., I want..., So that...), Story Points, Team Velocity.

Chapter 4 • Day 4–5 PPTs + Textbook Ch. 5 & App. 5A

Databases, Integrity, Crow’s Foot ERDs & Normalization

Page 6 of 15
CN7039 Day 4–5 PPTs & Cinema ERDTextbook 8e Ch. 5 & App. 5A (1NF–3NF)

Entity Integrity: Primary Key cannot be NULL or duplicated. Referential Integrity: Foreign Key must match an existing Primary Key in the parent table (deleting Khalil leaves orphan BookLoans unless cascaded). FK ALWAYS lives on the MANY side of `1:M`.

  • Optionality (min 0/1) & Cardinality (max 1/many): || (exact 1), O| (0 or 1), |< (1+), O< (0, 1, or more).
  • M:N Resolution: Insert an Associative (Intersection) Entity holding FKs of both parents (`Proj_Emp`, `ORDERED PRODUCT`, `ORDERED DRONE COMPONENT`).
  • [Textbook Ch. 5] Independent vs Dependent Entities: Single-border box (own PK) vs Double-border box (borrows parent PK as part of its PK = identifying relationship).

[Textbook App. 5A] 3 Steps of Normalization (Employee File → 3NF):

  • 1NF (First Normal Form): Remove repeating attributes / repeating groups into new entities, and split composite attributes (`Home_Address` → Street, City, State, Zip).
  • 2NF (Second Normal Form): Must be 1NF + remove partial dependencies (every non-key attribute must depend on the whole concatenated key, not just part of it — e.g. `Skill_Description` depends only on `Skill_ID`, not `Emp_ID + Skill_ID + Date`).
  • 3NF (Third Normal Form): Must be 2NF + remove transitive dependencies (non-key attributes cannot depend on another non-key attribute — e.g. `Skill_Level_Name` depends on `Skill_Level_Code`, move to `SKILL_LEVEL`) and remove derived attributes (don’t store `Age` if `Birthdate` is stored).
  • [Textbook Ch. 5] CRUD Matrix: Balances ERD with DFD—verify every attribute is Created, Read, Updated, Deleted by some process!

Data Flow Diagrams, Conservation of Data & Balancing

Page 7 of 15
CN7039 Week 6 PPTs & Topic 6Textbook 8e Ch. 4 (DFD Rules)
Notation Bridge (Don’t Get Tricked!):
• CN7039 Drawn Slides (Wk 6 Slides 4–5): External Entity = Oval; Process = 3-Part Box (Sequence #, Person/System Responsible, Verb Action); Data Store = D1 | Name.
• Textbook Figures (Dennis et al. / PPT Slides 8, 12, 22, 27): External Entity = Shadowed Rectangle; Process = Rounded Rectangle (Number + Verb Phrase). Both follow the exact same rules!

[Textbook Ch. 4] Prof. Dale Goodhue’s Law of Conservation of Data:
1. Data at rest stays at rest until moved by a process (No Entity↔Entity, Store↔Store, Entity↔Store direct flows!).
2. Processes cannot consume or create data (No Black Hole/Sinkhole with 0 outputs; no Miracle with 0 inputs; no Grey/Impossible hole missing needed inputs—to make H2O you need BOTH H and O!).

  • Context Diagram (`0` / `0.0`): 1 process for entire system, external entities, NO Data Stores.
  • Level 0 (`1, 2, 3...`): Explodes `0` into 3–7 processes + Data Stores. Net external inputs/outputs MUST balance with Context!
  • Level 1 (`1.1`) & Level 2 (`1.1.1`): Periods in number = DFD level! Stop decomposing when lowest process = 1 functional primitive (~25–50 lines of logic).
  • No Two-Headed Arrows: Read and write to a Data Store must be two separate one-way arrows.
  • Consistent Viewpoint & Verb Rule: Output flow name must differ from input flow name; process verb must match what it does (`Calculate and save grades`).
Chapter 6 • Week 7–8 PPTs + Textbook Ch. 9, 11–12

OOP, Class Diagrams, Cohesion/Coupling & Conversion

Page 8 of 15
CN7039 Week 7–8 PPTsTextbook 8e Ch. 9 (Cohesion/Coupling) & Ch. 12

Class = template (`[Name | -Attributes | +Methods()]`). Object Instance = concrete item (`412209 : Customer`). Encapsulation bundles data + behavior together. Polymorphism & Override let subclasses respond differently to the same method (`computePay(hours)`).

  • Association (`──`): General link with multiplicity (`1`, `0..1`, `0..*`, `1..*`).
  • Aggregation (`◇──` on Whole): Weak "has-a"; parts survive if whole dies (`Team ◇ Player`).
  • Composition (`◆──` on Whole): Strong "has-a"; whole creates & destroys parts (`Book ◆ Chapter`).
  • Inheritance (`──▷` to Parent): "is-a" (`SpecialOrder ──▷ Order`).
  • [Textbook Ch. 9] Cohesion (Best → Worst): Functional (1 task only—best!) → Sequential → Communicational → Procedural → Temporal → Logical → Coincidental (unrelated—worst!). Fix bad cohesion via Factoring (split into separate modules).
  • [Textbook Ch. 9] Coupling (Best → Worst): Data (pass only needed fields—best!) → Stamp (whole record) → Control (flag passed) → Common (global data) → Content (refers inside another module—worst!). Aim for high fan-in (reusable modules) & low fan-out (≤7 subordinates).
  • [Textbook Ch. 12] Lewin’s Change Model: Unfreeze → Move (Migration Plan) → Refreeze. Adopters: 20–30% Ready, 40–60% Reluctant, 20–30% Resistant.

How to Decode & Draw All 4 Diagrams From Scratch

Page 10 of 15

Never start drawing randomly! Follow these 4-step Decode & Draw Recipes combining CN7039 exam rules with Dennis et al.’s systematic methods:

1. UML Use Case Diagram (Decode & Draw)

  • How to Decode in 30 Seconds: Find the Boundary title (what system is this?) → list Actors outside (confirm no DB/Printer!) → read every oval (does it start with a verb?) → check `«include»` (arrow points OUT to mandatory helper) vs `«extend»` (arrow points IN from optional add-on).
  • How to Draw from Scratch: (1) Draw System Boundary rectangle + title; (2) Underline external nouns in the case study (people, external systems, time) = Actors outside; (3) Underline user goals (verb + noun) = Ellipses inside; (4) Connect Actors to their primary Use Cases; (5) Factor out shared mandatory steps with `«include»` (Base → Helper) and optional/conditional steps with `«extend»` (Extension → Base); (6) Group shared roles with `──▷` Inheritance (Child → Parent).

2. Entity Relationship Diagram — ERD (Decode & Draw)

  • How to Decode: Read entities as tables → check `*Identifier/PK` → read each line in both directions (`A` has `min..max` of `B`; `B` belongs to `min..max` of `A`) → verify no raw `M:N` lines remain → verify FKs sit on the MANY side!
  • How to Draw from Scratch (Textbook Ch. 5 + CN7039): (1) List major nouns with multiple instances = Entities; (2) Add attributes + choose Identifier/PK (avoid composite full-name PKs, avoid storing derived fields like Age); (3) Draw lines and ask two questions for every pair: "How many max?" (Cardinality: `1` or `<`) and "Can one exist without the other?" (Modality/Optionality: `O` if yes, `|` if no); (4) Resolve every `M:N` by inserting an Associative Entity between them; (5) Copy parent PKs into child tables as FKs; (6) Quick 3NF check (no repeating groups, no partial dependencies, no transitive dependencies).

3. Data Flow Diagram — DFD (Decode & Draw)

  • How to Decode: Start at leftmost External Entity → trace arrows through numbered Processes (`1`, `2`, `3`) to Data Stores (`D1`) → verify every process has ≥1 valid input AND ≥1 output (no Miracle, no Black Hole, no H2O missing-ingredient Grey Hole) → verify zero Entity↔Entity, Store↔Store, or Entity↔Store direct lines.
  • How to Draw from Scratch (Textbook Ch. 4 + Week 6): (1) Write an Event–Response List from the text; (2) Draw the Context Diagram (`0`, External Entities, boundary data flows, zero Data Stores); (3) Create one DFD Fragment per major event/use case (add Data Stores needed to supply/save data); (4) Assemble Level 0 (`1..n`, top-left to bottom-right) and Balance every external flow back to the Context Diagram; (5) Explode complex processes into Level 1 (`1.1`, `1.2`) and Level 2 (`1.1.1`) if needed.

4. UML Class Diagram (Decode & Draw)

  • How to Decode: Check 3 compartments (`Name | -Attributes | +Methods()`) → identify `is-a` triangles (`──▷` points to Superclass) → identify `has-a` diamonds (diamond touches WHOLE: `◆` = dies together, `◇` = survives independently) → read multiplicities (`1`, `0..*`, `1..*`).
  • How to Draw from Scratch: (1) Identify business classes from nouns; (2) Assign `-private` attributes and `+public()` behaviors (encapsulation); (3) Spot shared attributes/methods to pull up into a Superclass (`Person` ← `Customer`, `Manager`); (4) Connect remaining classes with Associations + Multiplicities; (5) Upgrade Whole–Part links to Composition (`◆`) if part dies with whole, or Aggregation (`◇`) if part survives independently.

Story A: DrōnTeq Client Flight Services Textbook 8e Ch. 1–12

The Plot: Commercial drone maker DrōnTeq launches a new business unit connecting Clients needing aerial surveys with licensed Drone Pilots who bid on flight requests.

1. Use Cases (`UC-2A`, `UC-2B`, `UC-2C`): Client submits a flight request (`External Trigger`) → `UC-2A Notify Pilots` (75-mile radius, expands +25 miles if none found) → `UC-2B Pilot Submits Bid` → `Bidding Window Closes` (`Temporal Trigger`) → `UC-2C Select Winning Flight Bid` (Flight Operations Manager checks drone sensors like infrared and picks winner).
2. Process Model (DFD Ch. 4 & 9): Fragment `2 Assign Flight Request to Pilot` explodes into Level 1 (`2.1`, `2.2`, `2.3`) and Level 2 (`2.1.1`–`2.1.4`), reading `D1 Flight Requests`, `D2 Pilots`, `D3 Pricing Guidelines`, and writing `D4 Flight Request Notifications` & `D5 Flight Bids`. In Design (Ch. 9), it becomes a Physical DFD (`MySQL: Parts Request Table`, `Python`, dashed Human–Machine boundary, `D6 ASCII: Audit File`).
3. Data Model (ERD Ch. 5 & 10): `CLIENT` (`*CLI_ID`) `||──O<` `FLIGHT REQUEST` (`*FRQ_ID`) `||──O<` `FLIGHT REQUEST NOTIFICATION` (`*FRN_ID`). `FLIGHT BID` is a dependent entity (double border) identified by `*FLN_ID + *PIL_ID`. Verified balanced via a CRUD matrix!

Story B: NovaCare Clinic (All 4 Core Diagrams Connected)

• Use Case: `Old Patient` & `New Patient` → `Patient` → `Make Appointment` (`«include»` `Verify Patient`; extended by `Update Details`). External actor: `Bank Payment System`.
